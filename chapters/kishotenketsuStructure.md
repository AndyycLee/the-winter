### Seven methods to copy

1. **The first sentence is the premise.** "I have a childhood friend who I've never met" (p. 4). Chapter 2 ends with the same paragraph in the past tense (p. 44). Chapter 8 opens with it again from her side: "I've never seen his face" (p. 155).
2. **Many chapters open with a short essay.** Chapters 3, 4, 5, 6, and 9 begin with a few paragraphs of reflection before any scene. The closest model for your rant is the start of Chapter 6: "Nightmares are kind."
3. **The Ten repeats scenes from the other side.** The shrine meeting, the food in the trash, and the typhoon night each occur twice. The Ten adds almost no new plot.
4. **Short side stories test wrong answers.** Okano and Ikeda (a scam). The Kayano sisters (one person plays another). "The Man Who Mistook His Wife for a Substite" (the memories were real). Each one offers an answer to the main question. None of them is the true answer.
5. **Small objects return late.** On p. 76 he "prepared a little trick". The trick decides a scene on p. 247. On p. 72 his father gives him an unsigned letter. On p. 254 one clause suggests who wrote it: Nozomi, "the one person who thought of me in middle school."
6. **The older narrator admits regret without facts.** "She cried by herself every night" (p. 124). The reader learns that something is wrong, but not what.
7. **The last chapter is short.** Chapter 12 has 2,259 words. It gives the fates of the people around him, then the address to "you", then one scene from Chapter 1 again.

Strong points in your plan:

- **The double life hides the truth.** From his side it needs an ordinary reason, and the reader will supply one: she does not want her friends to see her with him. That reason is painful, ordinary, and wrong. The true reason is that she protects him from the day she forgets.
- **"Huh, it is a nice view" repeats three earlier lines.** The dawn girl says "Huh." on day 1. In the past life the boy says "It's a good spot, huh" and "I just liked the view." On a first read it is small talk. On a second read it is an echo. The roles are also reversed: in the past life the boy came to the girl on the bridge.
- **Starting from scratch is your theme in action.** Chapter 1 is called "Zero". After the loss he starts from zero again with the same girl, and this time he takes the first step.
- **"Rebuilding friendships" is plural.** In the last months he learns to value people other than her. That is the part of your theme that Chihiro never reaches.
- **Your last scene reverses the first dream, point by point.** In Chapter 1 her hand is "ice". Now "she was warm". In Chapter 1 she calls to him from the yard. Now she walks up the aisle to him. In Chapter 1, "the longer I hold on," the more the dream fades. Now they let go by choice. The route is the same: right and down, then right again and up.
- **Your last line answers your first line.** "She's haunted me ever since" becomes "She has loved me ever since." It also matches the shape of Miaki's last line.

### 1. The dates

After the loss, about four to six months of school remain, depending on when your school year ends. That gives you the "like 4 months" of rebuilding.

These day numbers assume no February 29 between the dates. A leap day adds 1.

### 2. The bridge and the bus must not read as a fall

Your bridge scene ends with him close to the edge. Your bus scene opens with "when I opened my eyes", in hazy, peaceful, warm air. If the second scene follows the first directly, many readers will think that he jumped and that the bus is after death. That is the opposite of your theme. Put a clear break between the two scenes. For example:

- He walks away from the bridge, goes home, and sleeps. The bus scene starts in his sleep.
- Or the address to "you" sits between the two scenes. It proves that he lived.
- Or the bus scene is its own chapter, "Seven Hundred Thirty-Two", and its first line puts him in bed.

Chapter 1 already gives you the frame: "every time I close my eyes, I find myself back on that crowded bus."

### 3. Two one-word changes on the last page

Your last line changes one word of your first line: "haunted" becomes "loved". Your refrain plan also ends with one word changed, so that he acts. Two changes on one page can compete. Choose which one ends the book. You can put the other one earlier. For example, the refrain's change can come at the wave in Chapter 9, when he acts first.

Note also that the bus scene is silent: "we didn't say a word." That repeats "told no one" from the refrain, but now the silence is shared.

### 4. The letter explains

Your earlier rule says that the narrator never reaches a decision about who she is. A letter that explains the past lives gives him one. This can be right. The reveal has already happened for the reader by then, and Miaki's Chihiro also learns everything from a letter. If you want to keep some doubt, let him believe the letter without proof. She cannot confirm it, because she does not remember. Your last line, "a girl who doesn't exist", keeps that doubt.

### 5. Resignation and his hope

Your line: "maybe it was because I had resigned myself, that the good that came afterward didn't feel like a performance." Your earlier rule says that after each defeat his hope comes back, and he hates it. Both can be true. The resignation ends his performing: in Chapter 3 he "auditioned and discarded" versions of himself. It does not end his hope. Let the first visit be free of expectation. Then let the hope come back over the next days, with the shame.

### 6. Her visit is her choice

Your earlier rule wanted outside causes, such as a seating chart, to move the distance. Her visit is her own choice. It still works for two reasons. Its cause is already on the page: "Were you okay? Out there." And after the first visit, the bench is a fixed place and time, not a new decision each day.

### 7. The fence at her first visit

She must get past the fence at her first visit. Do not show how. He must not tell her about the gap, and he must not ask. If he does, "You told me. The patch in the fence." loses its force.

### 8. The rant

It is close to *Your Story* p. 42: "It's empty for an existing person to love a non-existent person, but it's equally empty for a non-existent person to love an existing person. And a non-existent person loving a non-existent person, that's just complete nothingness."

You can keep the homage. Your book also has its own reasons for the words:

- Chapter 1 says that a "net zero" is not part of the world: "You don't exist."
- Your first sentence says she "doesn't exist".
- From 9 to 3, the girl from the bench does not exist at school.

Build the rant from these reasons, so that it sounds like him and not like Chihiro. As the opening of Chapter 4, it follows Miaki's method: a short essay, then the scene. At that point the double life has not started, so use the first two reasons there. Bring back the third reason in Chapter 5 as one short line. This plants the image in full early and recalls it short later.
